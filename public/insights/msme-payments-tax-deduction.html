Tax

Paying small suppliers late now costs you tax

GrowLedger Advisors · November 2026 · 4 min read

Since the 2024-25 tax year, the income-tax law has linked your deductions to how quickly you pay small suppliers. If you buy from a micro or small enterprise registered under the MSMED Act and pay late, that expense is not allowed in the year you booked it. It is allowed only in the year you actually pay.

What counts as late

  • With a written agreement: the payment date agreed, but never more than 45 days from accepting the goods or services.
  • Without a written agreement: 15 days.

Why it matters

Say you owe ₹50 lakh to small suppliers on 31 March and those bills are already past the deadline. The ₹50 lakh is added back to your profit for the year. At a 25% tax rate that is about ₹12.5 lakh of extra tax, even though you will eventually pay the suppliers.

Medium enterprises and traders who are not registered as micro or small are outside this rule, so knowing which suppliers are covered is the first step.

What to do

  1. Ask every regular supplier for their Udyam registration and note whether they are micro, small or medium.
  2. Put written payment terms of 45 days or less in place with micro and small suppliers.
  3. Run a monthly ageing report of supplier dues, with MSME suppliers flagged.
  4. Before 31 March, clear any MSME dues that are past their deadline.

This insight is general information based on the rules as we understand them in November 2026. Rules change, so check the latest notifications or speak to us before acting.

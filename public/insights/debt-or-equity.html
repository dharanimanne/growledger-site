Debt or equity? A simple way to decide

GrowLedger Advisors · November 2026 · 3 min read

Both debt and equity can fund growth, but they cost you in different ways. Debt costs interest and needs regular repayment. Equity costs a share of ownership and future value.

Debt usually suits you when

  • The business has steady, predictable cash flows.
  • The money funds assets or working capital that pay for themselves.
  • You want to keep full ownership and control.

Equity usually suits you when

  • Cash flows are uncertain or still negative.
  • You need a large amount to grow quickly ahead of revenue.
  • An investor brings experience or access, not just money.

Questions to ask before deciding

  1. Can the business pay the EMI comfortably in a bad month?
  2. What will each option cost over five years, including dilution?
  3. What security or guarantees will a lender ask for?
  4. What rights will an investor want on decisions and exit?

Many businesses use a mix, for example equity for growth and a working-capital limit from a bank. A simple model of both options makes the choice much clearer.

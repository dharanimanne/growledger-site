Which products and customers actually make you money?

GrowLedger Advisors · November 2026 · 4 min read

Most businesses know their total profit. Far fewer know which products, customers or locations earn it. Often a few customers and products fund the rest, while others quietly lose money once every cost is counted.

Why the total is not enough

Gross margin on an invoice leaves out the costs that sit lower down: discounts and schemes, freight, extra credit days, returns, after-sales support and the team time a demanding customer takes. A customer with high sales and long credit can cost more than they bring in.

How we work it out

  • Start from your books. Split revenue and direct costs by product, customer and channel.
  • Allocate the hidden costs. Assign freight, discounts, credit cost and service effort on a fair, simple basis.
  • Rank the results. See contribution after all costs for each product and customer, from best to worst.

What you can do with it

  • Raise prices or reduce discounts where margins are thin, starting with the customers least likely to leave.
  • Change credit terms or minimum order sizes for customers who cost too much to serve.
  • Put sales effort and stock behind the products and customers that earn the most.
  • Stop or redesign the products that lose money.

Small changes, large effect

For a business with ₹25 crore of sales, a 2% better realised price adds about ₹50 lakh of profit, because almost all of it falls straight through. Knowing where to apply it is what makes the change safe.

Once set up, the same view can run every month in your MIS, so pricing and cost decisions are made on facts rather than feel.

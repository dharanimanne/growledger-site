Going global

Transfer pricing for India service centres: the basics

GrowLedger Advisors · November 2026 · 3 min read

When an Indian subsidiary provides services to its overseas parent or group companies, such as software development, support or back-office work, the price it charges is a transfer price. Indian tax law requires that price to be at arm's length, meaning what unrelated parties would agree.

How service centres are usually priced

Most capability centres use a cost-plus model. The parent pays the Indian entity its full operating cost plus an agreed markup. The markup has to be supported by a benchmarking study of comparable independent companies.

What you must do each year

  • Keep transfer pricing documentation that explains the services, the pricing method and the benchmarking.
  • Obtain an accountant's report on international transactions (Form 3CEB) and file it by the due date.
  • Make sure the agreement with the parent matches what actually happens.

Ways to reduce disputes

  • Safe harbour rules allow eligible service providers to accept a set markup and avoid detailed scrutiny.
  • An advance pricing agreement with the tax department can fix the method for several years.

Setting the model and agreement correctly at the start is far cheaper than defending it in an assessment later.

This insight is general information based on the rules as we understand them in November 2026. Rules change, so check the latest notifications or speak to us before acting.

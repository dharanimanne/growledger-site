Succession planning for family businesses: start before it is urgent

GrowLedger Advisors · November 2026 · 4 min read

Many family businesses plan succession only after a health scare, a dispute or a sudden death. By then, choices are made under pressure. Planning early lets the family decide calmly and put the answers on paper.

Separate three questions

  • Ownership: who will hold the shares, and in what proportion.
  • Management: who will run the business day to day, family members or professional managers.
  • Control: how big decisions are made, and how disagreements are settled.

Families often mix these up. A family member can own shares without running the business, and a professional CEO can run it without owning it.

Tools that help

  • A family constitution setting out values, roles, rules for the next generation joining, and how disputes are resolved.
  • A will for each promoter, so shares pass as intended.
  • A holding company or family trust to keep ownership together, where it fits the family's goals.
  • A shareholders' agreement covering transfers, exits and valuation.
  • A board with independent voices to bring outside discipline.

Mind the tax and legal side

Moving shares or assets within a family can carry income tax, stamp duty and company law consequences. Gifts, wills and trusts are treated differently, so check each step before acting.

Start with a conversation

A good first step is a facilitated family meeting to agree goals, followed by a written plan with timelines. Revisit it every few years as the family and the business change.
